A subset $K\subseteq \mathbb R^n$ is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded.
#### Proof
Suppose $K$ is compact. Then $f:K\to\mathbb R$ by $f(x)=||x||$ is [[Topology/Compact#Theorem\|bounded]], so $K$ is bounded. Furthermore, $K$ is [[Topology/Compact-closed\|closed]] in [[Topology/Hausdorff]] $\mathbb R^n$. 
Conversely, if $K$ is closed and bounded, find interval $[-M,M]$ s.t. $K\subseteq [-M,M]^n$. Then this space is a [[Topology/Tychonorff's Theorem on Finite Products\|finite product]] of [[Topology/Closed interval is compact\|compact intervals]] hence compact. But then $K$ is also [[Topology/Compact-closed\|compact]], as a closed subspace.

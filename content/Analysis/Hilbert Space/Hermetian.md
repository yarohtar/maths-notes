Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a [[Analysis/Hilbert Space/Hilbert Space]] and $T\in L(\mathcal{H})$ be a [[Analysis/Linear Operator]]. 
Then $T$ is Hermetian or self-[[Analysis/Hilbert Space/Adjoint]] if $T^{*}=T$
i.e. if $(Tx,y)=(x,Ty)$

### Proposition
For a complex [[Analysis/Hilbert Space/Hilbert Space]] $\mathcal{H}$ and a [[Analysis/Linear Operator]] $T\in L(\mathcal{H})$, 
there exist unique $T_{1},T_{2}\in L(\mathcal{H})$ Hermetian with $T=T_{1}+iT_{2}$.
#### Proof
$T_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(T+T^{*})$
$T_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(T^{*}-T)$

### Properties
- Eigenvalues of a [[Analysis/Hilbert Space/Hermetian]] operator are real.
- Two eigenvectors with different eigenvalues of a [[Analysis/Hilbert Space/Hermetian]] operator are orthogonal.
- The set of all eigenvectors of a [[Analysis/Hilbert Space/Hermetian]] operator forms a complete orthogonal set.
